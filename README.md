# Task 1: ADR Identification Basics

## Objective
To identify a potential Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) by reviewing the patient's symptoms, medication history, and timeline.

## Patient Case
- Patient: 45-year-old female
- Symptoms: Rash, itching, nausea, and dizziness
- Medication 1: Amoxicillin, started five days before the current assessment
- Medication 2: Paracetamol, taken as needed; dose and timing not provided

## Timeline
- Day 5: Amoxicillin started.
- Day 2: Symptoms began, approximately three days after starting amoxicillin.
- Day 0: Symptoms persisted at the time of assessment.

## Suspected ADR and Drug
Amoxicillin is the primary suspected drug. The onset of symptoms after treatment began supports a possible association. Rash and itching may suggest a hypersensitivity reaction. Nausea and dizziness are nonspecific and require further assessment.

## Assessment
The timing suggests a possible relationship between amoxicillin and the reported symptoms, but causality cannot be confirmed from the available information. Further details are needed about allergy history, previous exposure, paracetamol use, other medicines, alternative causes, treatment, and outcome.

## Conclusion
This is a suspected ADR potentially associated with amoxicillin. The case requires appropriate clinical assessment and documentation. The available evidence is insufficient to establish definite causality.
