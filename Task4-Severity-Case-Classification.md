1. Objective

To classify adverse drug reactions (ADRs) based on seriousness and expectedness. This task evaluates multiple cases to determine their clinical impact using pharmacovigilance principles.

3. Assessment of Seriousness

An adverse drug reaction is considered serious if it results in any of the following:

- Death.
- A life-threatening condition.
- Inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- A congenital anomaly or birth defect.
- Another medically important event requiring medical intervention to prevent one of these outcomes.

4. Assessment of Expectedness

An adverse reaction is expected when its nature and severity are consistent with the applicable reference safety information for the suspected medicine.

An adverse reaction is unexpected when its nature, severity, or specificity is inconsistent with that reference information.

5. Clinical Assessment

- Case 1: Skin rash and itching are classified as non-serious if no seriousness criteria are met.
- Case 2: Gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalization is serious.
- Case 3: Lactic acidosis is a potentially life-threatening reaction and is classified as serious.
- Case 4: Mild liver enzyme elevation is classified as non-serious based on the information provided, unless additional clinical details indicate otherwise.
- Case 5: Tendon rupture is a medically significant injury and is classified as serious in this case assessment.

Expectedness must be confirmed using the applicable reference safety information. A known reaction should not be classified as unexpected solely because it is severe.

6. Conclusion

Seriousness and expectedness are separate assessments in pharmacovigilance. An adverse drug reaction may be serious or non-serious and expected or unexpected. Accurate classification requires reviewing the patient information, reaction details, clinical outcome, and applicable product safety information.
