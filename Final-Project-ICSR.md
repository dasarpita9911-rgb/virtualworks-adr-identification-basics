B. Dechallenge

C. Alternative causes

D. Rechallenge

No information is available about recurrence following re-exposure. Rechallenge should not be performed solely to confirm causality when it could expose the patient to harm.

The timing of the reaction and reported recovery after discontinuation support a possible relationship. However, alternative causes have not been adequately evaluated, and important case details are missing. The causality category may change if additional information becomes available.

The WHO–UMC framework distinguishes a possible relationship from a probable/likely relationship partly by considering alternative explanations and the available withdrawal information.

8. Conclusion

This case demonstrates the four essential stages of an individual case safety assessment:

1. ADR identification: Identification of skin rash and itching following suspected amoxicillin exposure.
2. ICSR documentation: Structured recording of available patient, medicine, reaction, timeline, and outcome information.
3. Causality assessment: Evaluation of the temporal relationship, dechallenge, alternative causes, and rechallenge information.
4. Case classification: Separate assessment of seriousness and expectedness.

Accurate pharmacovigilance reporting requires complete documentation, appropriate follow-up, transparent handling of missing information, and application of established assessment criteria.
